BOREALIS Engine — LOX/RP-1 Species-Transport Validation

This reacting-flow case uses species transport with gaseous RP-1, a non-Sutherland ideal-gas mixing law, the k-ω turbulence model, and second-order upwind spatial discretization. The chamber operating point was calibrated to 2.10 MPa and 3020 K.

The CFD solution was compared directly with the BOREALIS isentropic and preliminary engine-sizing calculations. The table below shows both the analytical reference values and the calibrated CFD outputs so the reported percent differences are visible rather than presenting the CFD contours by themselves. The strongest agreement was obtained for thrust, with the remaining primary performance quantities generally within about five percent, except exhaust temperature.

GOX/Ethanol Pintle Injector — Spray-Angle Verification

The pintle-injector model was developed to verify the predicted spray angle and visualize the interaction between the oxidizer and fuel streams. The simulation combines the flame- temperature field with a streamfunction animation to show both thermal behavior and the underlying flow structure.

Verification should be presented using the measured or analytically predicted spray angle, the CFD-extracted angle, and the resulting percent difference. This makes the study the primary validation-focused case on the page rather than presenting contours alone.

Study 03 • Aerospike Flowfield

Aerospike Exhaust-Plume Study

This compressible-flow study examines the aerospike exhaust plume using Mach number, logarithmic streamwise Schlieren, and turbulent kinetic-energy contours. Together, the fields show the shock-cell pattern, plume-boundary development, and shear-layer mixing downstream of the spike.

The current results are presented as a flow-physics study. A future altitude sweep can extend the work by comparing plume adaptation, shock-cell spacing, and base-region behavior over a range of ambient pressures.

  • Mach-number plume development
  • Schlieren visualization of compression and expansion waves
  • Turbulent kinetic-energy distribution through the shear layers
  • Future altitude and pressure-ratio comparison
